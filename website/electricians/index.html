For electricians

Installation plans on the architect's floor plan

OpenECraft plans the electrical installation of a building: the floor plan as a scan or PDF under each level, socket outlets, switches and lights in the rooms, final circuits from the distribution board, and the board itself in 3D.

Try the live demo Open the web editor

From the floor plan to the distribution board

Every step is a command as well, so a plan can also be drawn by a script or an AI agent.

Floor plans on paper

A scan or PDF of the drawing goes under a level, to scale by measuring one dimension. Find rooms detects the walls, doors and windows, Make rooms turns them into rooms.

Outlets, switches, lights

A click in a room puts them on the nearest wall at their mounting height or on the ceiling, drawn with the symbols of installation plans after IEC 60617-11.

Final circuits

Every circuit starts at a protective device of the distribution board, the spare ones marked, and runs through its cable from item to item.

Cable length and voltage drop

Lengths along the installation zones after DIN 18015-3 and the voltage drop from the supply are worked out, checked against the rule sets and written into the cable list.

Circuits as a single-line diagram

The items become devices with tags in their rooms, and a generated single-line page shows every circuit with its protective device and cable.

Distribution boards

House connection boxes, distribution cabinets with component modules, metering with current transformers and PV feeders, in 3D with their busbars.

Engineering calculations

Conductor sizing, short circuit and selectivity, load calculation, EMC and safety functions after ISO 13849-1.

Start with an example

The examples include building services with water supply and PV storage, transformer-rated metering with PV and grid protection, and an office building with roof, stair and installation.

Before you switch

Find rooms works on walls drawn filled or hatched at right angles; on other drawings the rooms are drawn by hand. Cable lengths are estimates for planning, and a qualified electrician verifies the results against the regulations that apply.
