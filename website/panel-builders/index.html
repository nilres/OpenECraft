For panel builders

From the schematic to the wired control cabinet

OpenECraft carries a control cabinet from the device-oriented schematic through the panel layout in 3D into the workshop: mounting, wiring and the documentation for the customer, from one project file.

Try the live demo Open the web editor

What your shop gets

One project holds the schematic, the parts, the panel and the documents, so a change shows everywhere at once.

Device-oriented schematics

Coils and contacts form one device with live cross references and contact images, structure identifiers after IEC 81346, macros with variants.

Panel layout in 3D

Devices at real size on rails and plates, virtual wiring with duct fill levels, drilling data, enclosure climate, rooms and buildings.

Production data

Wire lists with routed lengths, stripping lengths and ferrules after DIN 46228-4, wires sorted for wire processing machines, drilling patterns and NC data of the mounting plates.

Wiring and mounting assistants

The routed wires as a checklist for the workshop, with the current wire in 3D, its colour, length and ferrule: a tablet with the web editor becomes a wiring station.

Climate of the enclosure

Heat loss after IEC/TR 60890 from the power loss of the devices, with filter fans, heat exchangers or cooling units, and a heat map of the mounting plate.

Terminals, plugs, PLC

Multi-level terminals, plugs and PLC cards with tag tables for TIA Portal, Studio 5000, TwinCAT, CODESYS and more.

Parts and data portal

A parts database with mounting data and 3D models, imports from EDZ, BMEcat and STEP, shared through the parts server.

Reports and checks

Bill of materials, terminal, cable and plug diagrams as live pages, own drawn forms and a project check that follows every change.

Teams and revisions

Shared projects with page locks and roles, released pages, approval workflow, comments and redlining.

Start with an example

The example projects include a conveyor with PLC and control cabinet, a pump station with structure identifiers, two bayed floor-standing enclosures with busbars and climate, and the main low-voltage distribution of an industrial plant. Open them in the live demo or in the web editor.

Before you switch

OpenECraft is young. Plan one real cabinet with it beside the tool you use today, and see what it does well and what you miss.

For a team, the parts server, the project server and the web editor run in your own network from Docker images, so the shop keeps its data.
